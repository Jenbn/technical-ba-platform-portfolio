# Work Item Templates

These templates reflect the work-item structures and working agreements used by the Platform Team I worked with as a Business Analyst. They helped us describe work clearly, support refinement and make relevant information accessible across the team.

## Templates

| Work item | When we used it | Template |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **User story** | New feature or capability work intended to create value for platform consumers | [User story](user-story.md) |
| **Bug** | Defective behaviour requiring separate tracking and development work | [Bug](bug.md) |
| **Support ticket** | Requests for help, troubleshooting, documentation, enablement or other support activities | [Support ticket](support-ticket.md) |
| **Spike** | Time-boxed investigation to reduce uncertainty and produce findings or a recommendation | [Spike](spike.md) |

## How We Handled Support Tickets and Bugs

Before raising a support ticket, stream-aligned teams were encouraged to consult our Shared Responsibility Model and FAQ. This helped clarify ownership, promote self-service, and reduce avoidable requests to the Platform Team.

A support ticket did not automatically become a bug. Our support pair first investigated the request and attempted to resolve it.

- **Resolved by the support pair:** The issue was addressed within the support work, without a separate bug ticket.
- **Medium- or low-priority defect requiring more work:** When the support pair could not resolve the defect directly, we created a separate bug linked to the original support ticket. The Product Owner assessed its priority for the backlog.
- **Critical production defect:** The support pair prioritised and worked on the fix immediately, rather than deferring it to a later sprint.
- **Defect found during active feature development:** The development pair handled the fix as part of the feature work.

These were our team's working agreements, not universal severity definitions. Criticality depended on impact and urgency, not simply the number of teams affected.

## How to Use the Templates

The templates provide a consistent starting point, not a requirement to fill every field for every item. The team adapted the level of detail to the type of work and its complexity.

For user stories and spikes, metadata included the team, PI, sprint, assignees, author and, where relevant, feature links and keywords. **Feature keywords** helped surface related Jira work items in Confluence feature documentation.
