# Platform Success Metrics

*How can a platform team tell whether its services make developers more effective—not just whether the team ships features?*

As a Technical Business Analyst in a platform team, I worked with support-ticket data, self-service usage metrics and developer feedback to identify friction, share insights and improve how stream-aligned teams used our services. This case study also documents measurement ideas I considered during the project but did not introduce.

## At a glance

- **Support demand:** Reviewed ticket classification and created eazyBI reporting by category, sprint and PI to identify recurring questions and responsibility gaps.
- **Self-service and documentation:** Improved FAQs, reorganised Confluence documentation, shared links in Slack and co-delivered a technical huddle.
- **Feature usage:** Regularly reviewed an existing temporary production DB access dashboard. Around 400 requests in one month during a go-live period helped illustrate demand for self-service.
- **Developer feedback:** Interviewed frequent users, requested wider feedback and shared findings with the PO and team; one out-of-scope analytical request was not pursued after limited broader demand was identified.
- **Reliable change:** The team's staged cluster migration completed with no observed downtime; it is included as an example of operational success, not as a personally owned KPI initiative.
  
## Evidence, boundaries and AI use

- The case study reflects my professional experience and recollections. It does not reproduce proprietary company dashboards or internal ticket data.
- The approximately 400 requests were observed in an existing dashboard, not independently verified here. This is not a count of avoided support tickets, unique users or hours saved.
- Improvements in support behaviour were observed informally, but their impact was not quantitatively established.
- Ideas discussed in the case study—including release-support comparisons, documentation impact and broader platform metrics—were considered, not implemented as a comprehensive measurement programme.
- AI assisted with organising and editing this portfolio narrative; it did not supply or validate the underlying professional observations.
